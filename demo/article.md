# Who Keeps the Water Running in a Town of 800

Most people never think about where their tap water comes from until it stops. In a small town, the answer is often one person with a truck, a pager and a binder of notes nobody else has read.

![a small-town water tower at sunrise](draftsman:pending)

## The job nobody applies for

A town of 800 can't afford a utility department. It has an operator, sometimes part time, who tests the water, fixes the leaks, reads the meters and files the state reports. When that person retires, the town learns how much of the system lived in one head.

The numbers show how thin the bench is:

| Town size | Operators on staff | Average years in the job | Share retiring within 10 years |
| :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Under 1,000 | 1 | 19 | 48% |
| 1,000 to 3,300 | 2 | 15 | 39% |
| 3,300 to 10,000 | 4 | 12 | 31% |

Small systems don't fail because the pipes are old. They fail because nobody is left who knows which valve sticks.

![an operator checking a pump station gauge](draftsman:pending)

## Where the money goes

The budget is just as thin. A small system spends most of what it collects keeping the lights on, and very little on what's under the street.

| Cost | Share of a small system's budget |
| :--- | ---: |
| Staff | 41% |
| Power for pumps | 22% |
| Chemicals and testing | 14% |
| Repairs | 15% |
| Saving for replacement | 8% |

At eight cents of every dollar, a pipe that should last 75 years gets replaced on a schedule closer to 200.

> The cheapest water system is the one where someone wrote down how it works.
>
> — A retired operator, after 31 years

![a hand-drawn map of a town's water lines](draftsman:pending)

## What helps

Towns that get through a retirement well do three things early: they pair the operator with a trainee for a year, they put the binder's contents somewhere others can find them, and they share an operator with the next town over before they're forced to.

## Summary

- Small water systems often depend on one operator who carries the system in their head.
- Nearly half of operators in towns under 1,000 retire within ten years.
- Most of a small system's budget goes to running it, little to replacing it.
- Pairing a trainee, writing things down and sharing operators help most.
