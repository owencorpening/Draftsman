# Pull quote criteria (sample)

Copy this file to `.draftsman/criteria/pullquotes.md` in your project and edit it to make it yours.

A good pull quote:

- Stands on its own. A reader who sees only the quote understands it, with no "this", "they" or "it" pointing at something earlier.
- Makes a claim or a turn, not a summary. It's the sentence someone would underline.
- Is short: one or two sentences, under about 30 words.
- Is the author's own words, not a quote of someone else's, unless that quote is the point.
- Doesn't give away the ending or the answer the article builds to.
- Isn't a number on its own. A number belongs in a table or chart; a pull quote says what it means.

How many:

- About one per 600 words, and never two in a row without a section between them.
- None in the first two paragraphs.

Always offer more candidates than will be used, say why each one works, and let the writer pick.
